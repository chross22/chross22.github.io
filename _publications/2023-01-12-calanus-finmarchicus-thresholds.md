The planktonic copepod *Calanus finmarchicus* is a fundamental prey resource for the critically endangered North Atlantic right whale (*Eubalaena glacialis*). Rather than modeling *C. finmarchicus* abundance broadly, this study models the environmental conditions associated with prey densities that exceed nominal right whale feeding thresholds ("τ-patches"), since whales forage based on whether prey aggregations exceed an energetic threshold rather than on abundance alone. Using a random forest model with bathymetry, temperature, salinity, day of year, chlorophyll, and current covariates, model performance was highest at a threshold of 10,000 copepods m⁻². High-density aggregations showed different environmental associations than previous abundance-based models, including a warmer favorable temperature range and higher spatial variability. The resulting prey field is designed to link directly with existing right whale density models used in US governmental decision support tools.
